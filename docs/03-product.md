# Product: Liquid-Core Burst Candy (Warheads-style)

## Concept

Hard/shell candy pieces with a **liquid center that bursts on bite** (Warheads/Gusher sensation) carrying the actives. Serving = 3–5 pieces chewed on the way into the gym.

## Why burst beats plain gummy

1. **Dose headroom is much better.** The liquid core carries concentrated actives with no gelatin/sugar bulk. Pharma softgels fit 200mg caffeine in a volume far smaller than a candy piece, and a US Amazon product (Energy Rocks popping candy) already ships **100mg caffeine + L-theanine per serving**. A 150–200mg/serving burst candy is realistic.
2. **The gummy taste-ceiling problem is solved by the split.** Liquid-center tech (e.g., Rousselot's SiMoGel gelatin systems, built to separate actives from the shell) hides bitter caffeine in the core while the shell stays sweet/sour candy. Plain gummies cap caffeine at ~25–75mg/piece purely on taste; burst format doesn't.
3. **The sensory hit IS the marketing.** A mouth-burst is a native TikTok reaction shot. Warheads-level sour doubles as the "this is working" ritual — same psychology as beta-alanine tingles, which gym bros actively love.
4. **Perceived speed.** Bite → burst → immediate flavor/caffeine hit reads as "fast-acting" next to a gummy you chew for 10 seconds.

## Dose reality for the must-haves (beta-alanine + L-theanine)

Brief requirement: **beta-alanine + L-theanine minimum, plus caffeine.**

- **L-theanine: fully fits.** 200mg/serving (1:1 with caffeine) dissolves cleanly in the liquid core, mild taste. ✅
- **Beta-alanine: fits at a meaningful dose, not the clinical dose.** Carnosine loading is 3.2g/day — that never fits in candy (a burst piece's core holds hundreds of mg of actives max). But **~250mg/piece × 4 ≈ 1g/serving is feasible**, and doses ≥~800mg reliably trigger the tingle (paresthesia) gym users treat as proof-of-work. Market precedent: plenty of pre-workouts ship 0.8–1.6g servings. Label the real number and disclose "may cause temporary tingling."
- **What still doesn't fit:** L-citrulline (6–8g), creatine (3–5g) — pump claims stay off the label.
- So the product remains an **energy/focus candy with pre-workout ritual branding** — but with beta-alanine on board it now has the tingle story for real.

## Format spec (draft)

| Attribute | Spec |
|---|---|
| Serving | 4 pieces = **200mg caffeine** + **200mg L-theanine** (1:1) + **~1g beta-alanine** (tingle ritual) |
| Beta-alanine | ~250mg/piece × 4 ≈ 1g/serving — enough to trigger the tingle (≥~800mg); the 3.2g clinical dose does NOT fit — label honestly + disclose tingling |
| Per-piece cap | ~50mg keeps a single stray piece non-dangerous (still: child-resistant packaging, docs/05) |
| Signature sensory | **Warheads-level sour shell** → sweet core. Brand mechanic idea: "sour levels" like heat levels (Level 1 → Nuclear) |
| Shell | Pectin or gelatin; core: glycerin/water solution with caffeine + theanine + flavor |
| Sugar | **v1.1: isomalt/maltitol + stevia (not allulose** — allulose is a reducing sugar and browns faster than glucose near amino acids; see docs/13). Shell v1 practice batches can still use sugar + corn syrup — actives sit in the core, separated |
| Packaging | 30-serving child-resistant jar + 12-count single-serve pouch packs (gym bag, sampling, creator seeding) |

## Manufacturing reality (the hard part)

- Plain gummy co-manufacturers number in the hundreds; **liquid-center/filled specialists are far fewer** — co-extrusion lines, SiMoGel-type licensed tech, many Asia-based exporters with US co-packers emerging.
- Expect **higher MOQ and cost than plain gummies**: budget ~**$10–25k first run** (vs $5–15k), longer lead times, more sample iterations.
- **Fallback MVP:** white-label caffeine softgels (200mg liquid gels are standard) in candy-branded packaging — proves demand fast but loses the candy magic. Use only as a demand test, never as the final product.

## What NOT to claim

- Never "replaces your powder pre-workout" (dose reality will get you review-bombed)
- No disease/medical claims; structure/function only
- Never candy-coded marketing to minors — this behaves exactly like candy while holding a caffeine liquid core (see docs/05; kid-risk is the #1 compliance issue for this format)
