# Risks & Compliance (US market)

## Regulatory — dietary supplement baseline

- **DSHEA framework:** sold as a dietary supplement. Requires a **Supplement Facts panel**, serving/servings-per-container, and a **structure/function claims only** policy ("supports energy/focus/endurance") — no disease claims, no "treats/cures" language. The standard FDA disclaimer goes on the label.
- **Facility + GMP:** product must be made in an FDA-registered, cGMP-compliant (21 CFR 111) facility — this is what your contract manufacturer provides; verify their registration and get batch records.
- **Label claims on caffeine:** state mg per serving clearly; FDA has warned the industry about pure/highly concentrated caffeine products — gummies at ≤25mg per gummy are on the safe side of that line.
- **FTC:** advertising + influencer posts need substantiation and #ad disclosure; keep claims in ads identical to label claims.

## Safety — the kid problem (real and getting attention)

Caffeine candy looks and behaves like candy — and a **liquid core that bursts in the mouth is maximum kid appeal while holding concentrated caffeine**. Pediatric-exposure literature on energy products is growing (2025 review of poison-center data; AAP position: no safe level of energy drinks for kids). Regulators and marketplaces are watching this exact product shape.

**Mitigations (non-negotiable for this format):**
- **Child-resistant packaging** (push-and-turn jar, not a candy bag) + "KEEP OUT OF REACH OF CHILDREN"
- "Not for individuals under 18" on label and every ad
- Cap caffeine per piece (~50mg) so a single accidental piece isn't dangerous
- Never use candy-lookalike imagery or youth-coded creators in ads
- Sour shell = acid: expect enamel-erosion chatter; sugar-free + "rinse after" guidance reduces it

## Marketplace requirements

- **Amazon:** supplements need documentation (CoAs, GMP proof) or listings get suppressed; third-party lab CoA per batch is effectively mandatory.
- **TikTok Shop:** has its own supplement onboarding rules — budget lead time for approval.
- **California Prop 65:** heavy-metal limits apply to gummies; ask manufacturers for Prop 65 compliance docs up front.

## Operational risks

| Risk | Reality | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **Melting** | Gummies melt in hot cars/mail — summer DTC shipping is the classic gummy killer | Pectin + high melt-point formula; heat warnings; summer cold-pack surcharge |
| Dose consistency | Actives can settle unevenly in gummy batches | Manufacturer with in-process testing; retain samples per batch |
| Clumping/sticking | Gummies fuse in heat | Quality coating; desiccant; single-serve pouches |
| Copycat risk | Format is hot; incumbents (Bucked Up) already entered | Move fast on niche community + subscription lock-in |
| Chargebacks/subscriptions | Supplement subs attract churn + disputes | Clear cancellation, dunning emails |

## Insurance / legal

- Product liability insurance (supplement-specific) before first sale — ~$1–3k/yr
- LLC + trademark search on name before printing labels (USPTO TESS)
- Consider a supplement-industry regulatory consultant for label review ($500–1,500 one-time) — cheapest insurance in this business
